Why ProbeStep exists

By Jared Chu · November 26, 2026

ProbeStep started with a familiar stall. The agent had tried several plausible things. The work still had the same symptom, and the next suggestion sounded like a repeat.

  1. Keep what the failed attempts taught

    The first version was an unblock checklist. Its checkpoint gained a specific job over time. Record what was tested, the conditions, what happened, and where the next session can find the evidence. A ruled-out explanation is useful only within the conditions that ruled it out.

  2. Ask which result would change the decision

    That rule also applies before a commitment. ProbeStep became a standalone skill for either moment. The checkpoint names the smallest check that can separate the remaining explanations, and explains why its scope is enough.

  3. Take the ties seriously

    In the earlier Codex pilot, the available skill and the baseline reached equally good decisions across all six cases. Replaying the same cases at other reasoning settings yielded the same result. The preview recommends explicit invocation while its benefit on new tasks remains open. Read the matched results.

  4. Make the stop rule testable

    A receipt certifies the step it acknowledges. Claims about the requested effect need evidence of that effect. Mandatory checks keep their place when discretionary probing pauses. The preview also carries the count across handoffs and asks the owner to decide whether to continue after the third unresolved probe.

ProbeStep remains an experiment in how to make progress visible when the next action is unclear. The notebook records the tests alongside the decisions they did and did not settle.
